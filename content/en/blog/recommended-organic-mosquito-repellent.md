## Organic mosquito repellent: the 2026 comparison of four certified brands

The natural repellent shelf has doubled in volume over ten years, and it has become hard to read. Two neighbouring bottles can carry the same claims, the same green leaf, the same "lemon eucalyptus" in large type, and yet one holds for six hours and the other for forty minutes. The difference lies neither in the price nor in the label, but in one line of the ingredient list that almost nobody reads.

## What "organic" actually covers on a mosquito repellent

This is the point buying guides handle least, and the one that explains most of the disappointment in use.

### A skin repellent is a biocide, not a cosmetic

A repellent intended for skin falls under European Regulation 528/2012 on biocidal products, in category PT19 covering repellents and attractants. In France, its active substances are assessed by ANSES, the national health and safety agency. A tube of moisturiser and a bottle of repellent are therefore judged on different criteria: the latter must demonstrate measured repellent effectiveness, the former does not.

Four active substances dominate this framework for human use today: DEET, IR3535, icaridin and PMD, also known as citriodiol. The first three are synthetic in origin and cannot enter an organic-certified formula. PMD is obtained from a plant. It is mechanically the only recognised active substance able to coexist with an organic label, which narrows the field of serious candidates considerably.

### What the label certifies, and what it does not

A standard such as Cosmos Organic, Ecogarantie or COSMOS covers ingredient origin, farming methods, permitted processing and the absence of certain families of compounds. It does not cover the repellent performance of the finished product.

The consequence is direct: a repellent can be faultless on certification and still hold for forty minutes on skin. Conversely, an organic-certified citriodiol product holds for several hours because its active has been concentrated, not because it is labelled. Both pieces of information are useful, they simply answer different questions. The label answers "what is it made from", the active substance answers "how long am I protected".

## Citriodiol or essential oils: the only two credible organic families

The natural shelf mixes products that do not belong to the same technical category. Telling them apart requires understanding a process, not comparing packaging.

### Citriodiol, a transformed lemon eucalyptus oil

Raw lemon eucalyptus oil contains citronellal, a weakly repellent compound. Hydrodistillation transforms it by concentrating PMD, the molecule that genuinely drives mosquitoes away. The result carries the trade name citriodiol, and its INCI name is explicit: "eucalyptus citriodora oil, hydrated, cyclized".

Que Choisir issued the clearest warning on this point in its analysis of natural repellents:

> "Check that the wording 'hydrated cyclized', which indicates this transformation, or the trade name (citriodiol) does appear in the ingredient list. It should not be confused with lemon eucalyptus essential oil, whose composition is different and whose effectiveness is far lower."
> Que Choisir, 6 June 2025

In other words, two products can both claim lemon eucalyptus and have nothing in common. Reading three words on the label settles the question better than any online review.

### Essential oil blends, effective but volatile

Mixtures of Java citronella, rose geranium, lavandin or lemongrass do have real repellent action. Their limit is physical: these molecules evaporate quickly, and protection collapses with them. Que Choisir sums the phenomenon up in one phrase, "essential oils: too volatile". In practice, staying power frequently falls below one hour, which makes the approach workable for an evening on a terrace and much less so on a hike. Our detailed review of [natural mosquito repellent sprays](/en/blog/natural-mosquito-repellent-spray-best-brands/) sets out the profile of each.

| Family of actives | Origin | Indicative protection time | Compatible with an organic label |

## Pranarôm, the only organic range combining both approaches

The Belgian house has worked in aromatherapy since 1991, and its mosquito range is built on a combination rather than a choice: citriodiol delivers the duration, the organic essential oil blend broadens the repellent spectrum. That is what sets it apart from brands that stayed with essential oils alone.

### What the products state

- Repellent active: Citriodiol® combined with repellent organic essential oils
- Certification: Ecogarantie, issued by the Belgian body Certisys
- Body spray: up to 7 h of protection against mosquitoes
- 75 ml body roll-on: up to 11 h of effectiveness, mosquitoes and ticks, from 3 years
- Full range: body spray, roll-on, fabric spray indicated from 3 months, after-bite roll-on
- Prices observed on the brand's site: 13.50 € to 22 € depending on the reference

The duration gap between the spray and the roll-on deserves an explanation, because it often surprises. A roll-on lays down a thicker, less dispersed film than a spray, which slows evaporation of the active. With a similar formula, format therefore changes staying power as much as concentration does. The brand's [mosquito and tick range](https://www.pranarom.fr/collections/anti-moustiques-et-tiques) details the indications for each reference.

The weak point of this range is its minimum age on skin, set at 3 years. For a younger child, the brand points to the fabric spray, which is consistent in terms of tolerance but leaves the skin uncovered. Families with an infant will find a more direct answer elsewhere.

## The three other organic brands on the shelf

### Florame, consistency in pure essential oils

The Provençal brand built its mosquito range on certified organic essential oils, with no DEET and no synthetic active, across a broad line-up: family lotion, roll-on, fabric spray, candle. Its positioning is legible and its Cosmos Organic certification solid. The trade-off is staying power, structurally limited by the volatility of essential oils, which means reapplying. It is a sound choice for occasional home use, less so for continuous exposure. On the quality of the essential oils themselves, our guide to [buying organic essential oils](/en/blog/where-to-buy-organic-essential-oils/) sets out the markers to read.

### Comme Avant, the only solid format on the shelf

The Marseille brand took a different route with a solid repellent based on murumuru butter and organic candelilla wax, whose active is a PMD derived from Java citronella, this time transformed to concentrate the molecule. COSMOS certification covers the formula, and two points set it clearly apart: use is possible from 6 months, with a single daily application until age 2, and it is compatible with breastfeeding. It is the only one of the four to tick both boxes. The solid format does require manual application, which is less practical on the legs of a child who will not sit still, and the price per unit stays high.

### Puressentiel, brand awareness outside the organic scope

Stocked in almost every pharmacy, the brand states up to 7 h of protection across its anti-bite range, at around 14.50 € per 100 ml. Its formulas rely on essential oils, sometimes combined with a synthetic active depending on the reference, and are not organic certified. It has every place in a comparison of natural repellents, and far less in an organic selection. Its presence in "organic" rankings published online is one of the main sources of confusion on this market.

## Which organic repellent to recommend, by situation

### A baby or a child under 3

The Comme Avant solid stick is the only one of the four options usable from 6 months, at one application per day before age 2. The complementary approach, valid whatever the brand, is to treat clothing and the mosquito net rather than the skin: a fabric spray such as the one in the Pranarôm range is indicated from 3 months and avoids any skin contact.

### A pregnant or breastfeeding woman

Comme Avant explicitly states compatibility with breastfeeding, which the three other brands do not. For pregnancy, aromatherapy calls for medical advice before any application, since sensitivity to essential oils varies by trimester. Physical protection, long clothing and a mosquito net, remains the first line.

### A hike or a tick-prone area

The Pranarôm citriodiol roll-on is the only one of the compared products to claim documented action against ticks as well as mosquitoes, with stated staying power of up to 11 h. On this ground, a formula based on essential oils alone is not in the right category. The subject is covered in detail in our [natural mosquito and tick repellent](/en/blog/natural-mosquito-tick-repellent/) comparison.

### An evening on a terrace or in a garden

This is where pure essential oils perform best. A Florame lotion, a candle and reapplication every hour are enough to get through an evening, with a more pleasant scent profile than a concentrated active. The choice of the [best organic mosquito repellent spray](/en/blog/best-organic-mosquito-repellent-spray/) largely depends on this kind of use.

### A trip to a tropical area

The question changes in nature: it is no longer about comfort but about preventing transmission. Medical advice before departure is the right course, and the choice of repellent should follow it, even if that means stepping outside the organic scope.

## The mistakes that make an organic repellent fail

1. **Confusing lemon eucalyptus essential oil with citriodiol.** Without the "hydrated cyclized" wording or the name citriodiol in the ingredient list, protection is not comparable.
2. **Spacing applications as with a synthetic product.** A formula based on essential oils alone needs reapplying every hour or two, not every six hours.
3. **Applying the repellent before sunscreen.** The reverse order is the right one: sun protection first, repellent afterwards, once the first has absorbed.
4. **Under-dosing exposed areas.** Ankles, wrists and the back of the neck attract the most bites and are the first to be missed.
5. **Treating skin rather than fabric on a very young child.** Before age 3, clothing and a mosquito net do the job with a far better tolerance margin.

One last marker, valid for all four brands: price per millilitre says nothing at all. A citriodiol bottle is applied two to three times less often than an essential-oil-only formula, so it lasts longer for the same protection. What should be compared is the cost per protected evening, not the cost of the bottle.
