## Antiperspirant and natural deodorant: two different actions

Confusion between the two categories explains most disappointments. An antiperspirant constricts the sweat ducts and lowers the volume of sweat released. A deodorant lets perspiration happen and targets the skin bacteria that break sweat down, which is what creates odour.

This distinction is not marketing, it is mechanical. Only aluminium salts, mainly chlorohydrates and chlorides, produce the constricting effect. Removing aluminium from a formula therefore removes the antiperspirant action, whatever the rest of the composition contains.

A product sold as a natural antiperspirant is almost always a natural deodorant. Across the five brands compared here, none claims a measured reduction in sweat flow, and the four brands selling a finished product label it «deodorant».

### Why the transition is often uncomfortable

After years of antiperspirant use, stopping aluminium salts returns sweat volume to its real level. The feeling of dampness increases for 2 to 4 weeks, without the natural product being at fault. Many people give up during that window.

## What the actives in natural antiperspirants are really worth

Four actives appear in almost every formula on the market. Their mechanisms differ widely, and two of them deserve closer reading than they usually get.

### Alum stone does contain aluminium

Natural alum stone is potassium alum, formula KAl(SO₄)₂, a double sulfate of potassium and aluminium. Its synthetic counterpart, ammonium alum, is produced industrially. In both cases, aluminium is present.

The «aluminium-free» argument attached to alum stone is therefore inaccurate. The regulatory claim «no aluminium salts» carried by Weleda, Sanoflore, Cattier and Respire excludes aluminium chlorohydrates, not alum. The difference lies in chemical form and solubility, not in the presence of the metal.

Its action remains astringent and purifying, which places it between a deodorant and a mild antiperspirant.

### Baking soda, effective on odour, irritating over time

Baking soda neutralises odour compounds through its basic pH, around 8.3. That is what makes it so effective on odour from the very first application.

That same pH creates a problem on skin, whose hydrolipidic film sits between 4.7 and 5.7. Redness and stinging under the arms after a few weeks of baking-soda deodorant is a common reason for dermatology consultations. This is why Respire and Sanoflore left it out of their formulas.

### Essential oils, the best documented active

Three essential oils appear consistently in aromatherapy protocols on this subject. Clary sage (*Salvia sclarea*) is the only one whose action goes beyond odour: its diterpenes act on the regulation of perspiration, which makes it the natural active closest to a true antiperspirant. Palmarosa (*Cymbopogon martinii*), rich in geraniol, targets bacterial flora. Tea tree (*Melaleuca alternifolia*) completes the purifying action, and [tea tree essential oil benefits](/en/blog/tea-tree-essential-oil-benefits/) in fact extend well beyond this use.

These three oils are used diluted at 2% maximum in a carrier oil, roughly 6 drops per 10 ml. Jojoba and apricot kernel suit the underarm area better than coconut, which solidifies below 24 °C. The carrier matters as much as the active, as the ranking of [organic vegetable oil brands](/en/blog/best-organic-plant-oil-brands-2026/) shows.

## Pranarôm, the individual essential oil approach

Pranarôm does not sell a finished deodorant. The Belgian brand, specialised in scientific aromatherapy since 1991, distributes chemotyped essential oils, meaning oils whose biochemical composition is analysed and documented batch by batch.

This positioning changes how perspiration is approached. Rather than a fixed formula, the [essential oil range](https://pranarom.fr/collections/huiles-essentielles) gives access to the actives separately, across 126 references in the catalogue, in a price range of 7.75 € to 97.35 € depending on the plant and volume.

### What this approach makes possible

- **Tuning the concentration**: reactive skin tolerates 1%, heavy sweating goes up to 2%, which no finished product allows.
- **Choosing the active by need**: clary sage when sweat volume dominates, palmarosa when odour dominates.
- **Checking traceability**: the chemotype names the majority molecule actually present, whereas a label reading «sage essential oil» says nothing about composition.

The trade-off is real: dilution happens at home, and there is room for error. Organic essential oils are not handled like a ready-to-use cosmetic, and [tea tree essential oil dangers](/en/blog/tea-tree-essential-oil-dangers/) apply to the other two as well.

## What actually separates the five brands

The price gap, from 6.50 € to 11.90 €, does not reflect a gap in odour control. The four formulated deodorants hold their 24 to 48 hour promise under normal conditions. The real deciding factor lies elsewhere.

On sensitive skin, Sanoflore takes the lead with an alcohol-free formula certified Ecocert Cosmos, presented as suitable for reactive underarms and heavy perspiration. Cattier plays on price, at 6.50 € for the 50 ml roll-on and 6.90 € for the 100 ml spray. Respire focuses on the solid format, with no plastic packaging and around 3 months of use for 50 g. Weleda holds a middle position, with essential-oil fragranced formulas at 9.90 €.

The aluminium debate deserves to be put back in context. France's medicines safety agency settled the substance of it in 2011:

> «No evidence establishes a link between cutaneous exposure to aluminium and the onset of cancer.» The agency nevertheless recommends restricting aluminium concentration in antiperspirant or deodorant products to 0.6%, and not applying them to broken skin or after shaving.
> Source: ANSM, 2011

In other words, switching to natural products is justified mainly by skin tolerance and everyday comfort, not by a demonstrated health risk.

## Which natural antiperspirant to choose by profile

### Moderate sweating, bothersome odour

A ready-to-use organic deodorant covers the need. Cattier at 6.50 € or Weleda at 9.90 € settle the question with no handling involved. The roll-on format holds better than a spray over a full day.

### Heavy sweating

No natural deodorant will reduce sweat volume. Clary sage remains the only serious avenue on the aromatherapy side, applied locally at 2% dilution, in the evening on clean dry skin. Beyond a settled daily nuisance, dermatological advice is required: hyperhidrosis calls for medical management.

### Sensitive or freshly shaved skin

Rule out baking soda and alcohol. Sanoflore ticks both boxes. Wait 24 hours after shaving before any application, whatever the brand, including for an essential oil dilution.

### Night sweats

A deodorant does not answer repeated night sweats, which are most often hormonal in origin. The issue is settled upstream, as covered in the guide on [natural treatment for hot flashes](/en/blog/natural-hot-flashes-treatment/).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Applying a neat essential oil under the arms.** The 2% maximum dilution is not a comfort precaution, it is the skin tolerance threshold.
2. **Using clary sage during pregnancy or breastfeeding.** It is also contraindicated in cases of hormone-dependent history.
3. **Judging a natural deodorant in three days.** The adjustment phase lasts 2 to 4 weeks after stopping aluminium salts.
4. **Expecting an aluminium-free product to keep skin dry.** By design, it will not.

| Active | Real action | Point of caution |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminium salts | Reduce sweat flow | Concentration capped at 0.6%, never on broken skin |
| Alum stone | Astringent and purifying | Contains aluminium despite the marketing claim |
| Baking soda | Neutralises odour | pH 8.3, irritating with prolonged use |
| Clary sage | Regulates perspiration | Contraindicated in pregnancy, breastfeeding, hormonal history |
| Palmarosa, tea tree | Purify the skin flora | 2% maximum dilution in a carrier oil |

One last marker, often overlooked: fabric. A polyester shirt holds the odour compounds that cotton and linen release in the wash. The best deodorant in this comparison will not compensate for a material that keeps the smell.
