Faced with a cold, sinusitis or a sore throat, knowing which essential oils to choose for an inhalation makes a real difference to the result. Some oils decongest within minutes, others soothe the throat or support respiratory defenses, and not all of them suit the same method or the same audience. This guide details the most effective essential oils for inhalation, the difference between steam and dry inhalation, usual dosages, and the precautions to know before starting.

## Why do an essential oil inhalation

Inhalation lets aromatic molecules act directly on the respiratory mucous membranes, without going through digestion. It is the fastest route for a local action on the nose, throat and bronchial tubes.

Three mechanisms explain the effect felt. **Oxide molecules** such as the 1,8-cineole in radiata eucalyptus thin out thick secretions and help clear them. **Monoterpenes** have a decongestant action on swollen mucous membranes. The **menthol** in peppermint activates cold receptors that produce an immediate sensation of fresh air, even without a real change in the diameter of the airways.

This approach usefully complements the basic measures against a cold: humidifying the indoor air, saline nasal rinses and adequate hydration. It does not replace medical treatment in case of high fever or symptoms that persist.

Essential oil inhalation is mostly sought out in winter, when colds, sinusitis and bronchitis follow one another in indoor air that is often too dry for the mucous membranes. Heating running continuously dries out the ambient air and weakens the natural defenses of the nose and throat, which explains why humidifying and inhaling work so well together over this period. Outside winter, the same method stays useful for occasional discomfort, an itchy nose at the end of the day, or a throat irritated after exposure to dust or smoke.

## Which essential oil to choose based on the respiratory issue

The choice of essential oil mostly depends on the symptom to treat as a priority: nasal congestion, cough, congested bronchial tubes or an associated ENT infection.

| Respiratory issue | Recommended essential oil | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| Cold, blocked nose | Radiata eucalyptus | Mucolytic, decongestant |
| Sinusitis | Radiata eucalyptus + ravintsara | Decongestant and antiviral |
| Congested bronchial tubes | Scots pine | Respiratory tonic |
| Sore throat, laryngitis | Tea tree, linalool thyme | Antibacterial for the throat |
| Viral cold | Ravintsara | Broad-spectrum antiviral |

### Radiata eucalyptus and Scots pine against colds and congestion

**Radiata eucalyptus essential oil** (Eucalyptus radiata) is rich in 1,8-cineole, a mucolytic molecule that thins nasal secretions and helps clear them. It is the best-tolerated oil in the group, usable from age 3 in diluted skin application, which makes it the first choice for **[essential oil to unblock the nose](/en/blog/essential-oil-unblock-nose/)** in inhalation as well as in skin application.

**Scots pine** adds a tonic effect on the respiratory tract, particularly useful for congested bronchial tubes. Its woody scent makes it pleasant in an evening chest rub, blended with radiata eucalyptus. For the more specific case of a **[runny nose](/en/blog/essential-oil-runny-nose/)**, other drying oils such as niaouli are sometimes better suited.

### Ravintsara and tea tree for ENT infections

**[Ravintsara](/en/blog/how-to-use-ravintsara-essential-oil/)** (Cinnamomum camphora ct cineole) is the most studied antiviral oil in aromatherapy. It pairs effectively with radiata eucalyptus for a cold of viral origin or a lingering ENT infection, and remains well tolerated from age 3 in diluted skin application.

**Tea tree** (Melaleuca alternifolia) adds a useful antibacterial action in case of a secondary infection or an associated sore throat. It is mostly used in a steam inhalation blend, rarely on its own, and never taken orally. When congestion mainly affects the bronchial tubes, **[essential oil to clear the bronchial tubes](/en/blog/essential-oil-clear-bronchial-tubes/)** details more complete blends built around these three oils.

## Peppermint for inhalation: when to use it

**Peppermint essential oil** (Mentha piperita) stands out for its immediate effect: one drop on a tissue, breathed in for a few seconds, is enough to give a sensation of fresh air that clears the nose. This effect comes from menthol, which acts on the perception of nasal airflow without actually changing the diameter of the airways.

This speed makes it the oil of choice for occasional relief, through direct inhalation from the bottle or on a tissue, rather than a prolonged steam inhalation. It is reserved for adults and teenagers over 12, and contraindicated for pregnant or breastfeeding women, children, people with epilepsy, and those with high blood pressure.

## Steam or dry inhalation: which method to choose

The two methods do not serve the same purpose. Steam inhalation acts in depth on the whole respiratory tract, while dry inhalation gives a flash effect, faster but more localized.

### How to do a steam inhalation step by step

Heat about one liter of water to 70°C, without boiling it: steam that is too hot destroys some of the active molecules. Pour it into a large bowl, add 2 to 4 drops of essential oil in total (for example radiata eucalyptus and ravintsara), then lean over the bowl, covered with a towel. Breathe deeply, eyes closed, for 5 to 10 minutes, once or twice a day.

This method is not recommended before age 6, for pregnant women, or for people with asthma, whose airways sometimes react poorly to concentrated steam.

### Dry inhalation, the fast and portable version

Simpler, dry inhalation consists of placing one or two drops of essential oil on a tissue, an inhaler stick, or directly at the bottle, then breathing deeply three to five times through each nostril. It can be repeated four to six times a day and suits travel or the office well.

Without heat or steam, it exposes users to fewer of the contraindications of steam inhalation, but remains reserved for children over 6 and adults for the most potent oils such as peppermint.

## How many drops and which recipe for inhalation

For an adult steam inhalation, the most common blend combines 2 drops of radiata eucalyptus, 2 drops of ravintsara and 1 drop of tea tree in a bowl of hot water. For a more targeted approach to nasal congestion alone, radiata eucalyptus can be used on its own, at a rate of 3 to 4 drops.

For children over 6, doses are halved and tea tree is generally left out in favor of the eucalyptus and ravintsara duo alone. The exact dosage also depends on the chosen route: the article on **[how to use radiata eucalyptus essential oil](/en/blog/how-to-use-eucalyptus-radiata-essential-oil/)** details proportions by application route, including skin dilution.

The equipment used also affects the result. A large bowl covered with a towel remains the most accessible and economical method, but a plastic or glass facial inhaler concentrates the steam better around the nose and limits losses from the sides. This kind of accessory also makes the position more comfortable than a bowl set on a table, particularly for an elderly person or a teenager repeating the inhalation over several days.

## Precautions and contraindications for essential oil inhalation

A few rules limit the risks tied to essential oil inhalation.

- **Children under 6**: steam inhalation is not recommended; dry inhalation stays reserved for mild oils and on the advice of a professional.
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding women**: most decongestant oils are contraindicated, especially in the first trimester.
- **People with asthma or epilepsy**: concentrated steam and some oils such as peppermint can trigger breathing discomfort or may not be suitable.
- **Duration of use**: no more than 10 minutes per steam inhalation, and no more than 5 consecutive days without medical advice.

A cold that persists beyond 10 days, a fever above 38.5°C, or associated facial pain should prompt a medical consultation rather than continued inhalations.
