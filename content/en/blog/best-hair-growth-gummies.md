## How to compare hair growth gummies without getting it wrong

The chewable format has reshaped the hair supplement aisle within a few years. Behind a uniform presentation, formulas differ radically, and the price printed on the jar says almost nothing about the real cost of a course.

Four criteria allow an objective comparison of the **best hair growth gummies**: the nature and dosage of the actives, the number of gummies required each day, the duration a single jar actually covers, and the price expressed per day of use.

Daily serving size is the most frequently overlooked criterion. Three of the four products compared require two gummies per day, which halves the apparent duration of the container. A 60-gummy jar then covers one month, not two.

The course duration recommended by the brands themselves ranges from two to three months, without exception in this comparison. Comparing prices on a monthly basis therefore means comparing fractions of a course, which systematically distorts the ranking.

### Why cost per day changes the ranking

Once tied to a day of use, the order of listed prices is overturned. Mium Lab offers the cheapest jar in the comparison at 21.90 euros, but its 42-gummy format covers three weeks at two gummies per day, pushing the daily cost to 1.04 euro, the highest of the four.

Bears with Benefits, conversely, shows a higher jar price at 26.51 euros, but a single gummy per day and formats running up to 180 days. The daily cost then falls to 0.43 euro, less than half that of Mium Lab.

## What a hair gummy is legally allowed to promise

European regulation tightly frames health claims, and that constraint explains most of the differences in messaging observed between brands.

The annex to Regulation (EU) No 432/2012 lists only six authorised claims relating to hair and nails. They cover four nutrients: biotin, zinc, selenium and copper.

> "Biotin contributes to the maintenance of normal hair"
> Regulation (EU) No 432/2012, annex of authorised health claims

Zinc and selenium carry equivalent wordings, covering the maintenance of normal hair and nails. Copper is the only nutrient with an authorised claim on normal hair pigmentation.

No authorised claim covers growth, thickness, density or hair loss. Any promise of that kind belongs to commercial messaging rather than to the regulatory framework, whichever brand makes it.

This grid is useful for sorting marketing arguments. Mium Lab announces accelerated growth and reduced hair loss, Luxéol longer hair and supported growth, Forcapil supported growth and longer hair. These wordings go beyond the authorised register. The messaging used by Bears with Benefits remains built around biotin and zinc, two nutrients genuinely covered by a maintenance claim.

One further condition applies: a claim may only be used if the product is at least a source of the nutrient concerned, meaning 15 % of the nutrient reference values for the daily serving. All four products meet that threshold for at least one hair-related nutrient.

## Bears with Benefits, the highest biotin dose in one gummy a day

The Munich-based brand, part of the Havea group, positions its hair range on a simple principle: one gummy a day, sugar-free and vegan. The Crinière de Feu Cheveux Sublimes product, in its sugar-free version, anchors the offering at 26.51 euros, down from 27.90 euros.

The formula delivers 10,000 µg of biotin per gummy, or 20,000 % of the nutrient reference values. That is the highest dose in the comparison, more than twenty times that of Forcapil. It is combined with vitamin C at 12 mg, vitamin E at 1.8 mg, vitamin B6 at 1 mg, zinc at 1.5 mg and iodine at 22.5 µg.

Two features stand out in practical terms. The single daily serving first, unique in this comparison, which simplifies adherence over a long course. The formats next, available in 45, 90 and 180 gummies, the 180-day version covering two complete course cycles.

Cost per day follows that format logic: 0.59 euro over 45 days, 0.53 euro over 90 days, 0.43 euro over 180 days. At all three tiers, the product remains the cheapest per day among the four compared.

The hair range also includes a sugar-containing version at the same price, a keratin-based product named Jusqu'au bout des ongles & cheveux, and a twelve-month course pack at 169.90 euros, down from 223.20 euros. The product page reports 262 customer reviews, 94 % of them five-star.

The brand targets women's health, with formulas free from gluten, lactose and GMOs, and not tested on animals. That orientation overlaps with the concerns covered in the comparison of brands dedicated to [female hormonal balance](/en/blog/female-hormonal-balance-natural-brands/), since hair loss is frequently linked to hormonal variation.

## Luxéol, horsetail and minerals instead of biotin

Luxéol holds a distinctive position in this comparison: its Gummies Force & Croissance formula contains no biotin at all. The 60-gummy jar sells for 24.90 euros, with bundles of three at 72.40 euros and of six at 140.40 euros.

Per two gummies, the formula delivers 50 mg of field horsetail extract, 12.5 mg of zinc or 125 % of reference intakes, 0.15 mg of copper and 82.5 µg of selenium or 150 % of reference intakes. It is the only product in the comparison containing copper, and therefore the only one able to claim an effect on normal hair pigmentation.

Zinc and selenium are dosed markedly higher than in competing formulas, respectively eight times and one and a half times the levels found at Bears with Benefits for the former. The approach favours mineral coverage over vitamin B8.

The product is sugar-free, using maltitol syrup as a sweetener, with a raspberry flavour and French manufacturing. Vegan status is not claimed on the product page, which lists carnauba wax as a coating agent. The brand shows a 4.6 out of 5 rating and reports more than 80,000 reviews across its range.

Daily cost stands at 0.83 euro for a single jar, falling to 0.80 euro with the three-jar bundle matching the three-month programme the brand recommends.

## Mium Lab, the only published clinical study in the comparison

Mium Lab is the only one of the four brands documenting a clinical trial on its product page. The sugar-free Gummies Cheveux et Ongles are offered at 21.90 euros per jar, or 19.71 euros on subscription with a 10 % discount.

The formula delivers 5,000 µg of biotin per two gummies, or 10,000 % of reference intakes, alongside vitamin B6 at 1.8 mg, vitamin B5 at 6 mg, vitamin B9 at 200 µg, vitamin B12 at 3 µg, plus zinc, vitamin C and vitamin D. The product is vegan, sugar-free and made in France.

The study referenced as ID024-21 ran over 90 days with 20 participants aged 18 to 70. It reports that 80 % of participants observed a significant reduction in hair loss and 75 % faster nail growth.

The effort deserves credit, but the sample size calls for caution. Twenty participants with no control group mentioned amounts to a satisfaction test rather than a controlled clinical trial, and the results rest on self-reporting.

The 42-gummy format is the main practical limitation. At two gummies per day it covers three weeks, which requires buying four jars to approach the three-month course the brand recommends, and pushes the daily cost to 1.04 euro.

## Forcapil, the pharmacy option with the lowest entry price

Forcapil, a brand of the Arkopharma laboratory, approaches the hair category from pharmacy distribution, with a range of nineteen products spanning capsules to serums. Gummies Croissance sells for 19.80 euros per box of 60 gummies, the lowest entry price in the comparison.

Per two gummies, the formula delivers 10 mg of horsetail extract, 40 mg of vitamin C, 6 mg of vitamin B5, 2 mg of vitamin B6, 446 µg of biotin or 892 % of reference values, 300 µg of vitamin B9, 5 mg of zinc and 55 µg of selenium. B-group vitamin coverage is the broadest of the four, but biotin is dosed more than twenty times lower than at Bears with Benefits.

The clearest difference concerns sugars. The product claims 30 % less sugar than the market average, which still leaves 2.3 g of sugars in the daily serving of two gummies, where the three other products are formulated without sugars.

The gummy is set with fruit pectin, without artificial colours or sweeteners, with a raspberry and red berry flavour. The brand recommends courses of two to three months and restricts the product to people over fifteen. A Gummies Anti-Chute variant is offered at 24.80 euros.

Daily cost stands at 0.66 euro, placing the product second behind Bears with Benefits on that criterion, while offering the most economical route to a one-month trial.

## Which gummy to choose by profile and objective

### For a long course with simple adherence

Bears with Benefits answers this profile most directly. The single daily serving removes the risk of missing a second dose, and the 180-gummy format covers two courses without reordering, at the lowest cost per day in the comparison.

### For a first trial on a contained budget

Forcapil Gummies Croissance offers the most accessible entry point at 19.80 euros for a full month, with pharmacy availability allowing in-store advice. The 2.3 g of sugars per day remains a consideration for anyone monitoring intake.

### For a mineral approach without biotin

Luxéol is the only option in this comparison built without biotin, around field horsetail, zinc, copper and selenium. Its zinc dose, at 125 % of reference intakes, and its copper content make it the most consistent choice for covering pigmentation as well as maintenance.

### For a course targeting nails as much as hair

Mium Lab documents results on both fronts, with 75 % of participants reporting faster nail growth. The constraint remains the three-week format, which forces frequent reordering.

## Mistakes to avoid before buying hair gummies

1. **Comparing jar prices without checking the daily serving.** Three products out of four require two gummies per day, which halves the duration of the container.
2. **Taking a growth promise for established fact.** No authorised claim covers hair growth, only the maintenance of normal hair.
3. **Stopping the course before two months.** All four brands recommend two to three months, since hair renewal produces no visible effect over a few weeks.
4. **Overlooking sugars on a long daily intake.** A daily dose of 2.3 g represents roughly 207 g of sugars across a three-month course.
5. **Forgetting to report biotin intake before a blood test.** The Food and Drug Administration updated a safety communication on 5 November 2019 regarding biotin interference with certain immunoassay laboratory tests, including troponin measurement. The high doses in this comparison fall within that scope.
6. **Treating the supplement as a standalone answer.** Persistent hair loss warrants medical advice and a search for the cause, whether iron deficiency, thyroid disorder or hormonal imbalance. A blood panel also helps establish a [vitamin D deficiency in adults](/en/blog/vitamin-d-deficiency-symptoms-adults/), frequently associated with general fatigue.
7. **Ignoring external care.** Nutritional intake does not replace products applied to the hair fibre, for which the [best organic vegetable oil brands](/en/blog/best-organic-vegetable-oil-brands/) offer simple and inexpensive alternatives.
