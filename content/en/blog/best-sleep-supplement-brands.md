## How to compare sleep supplement brands in 2026

The French sleep supplement market brings together very different logics under a single label. A melatonin-based formula and a cure of Buds do not pursue the same objective, are not used over the same duration and do not address the same type of difficult night.

Four criteria allow the **best sleep supplement brands** to be compared on an objective basis: the presence or absence of melatonin, the nature of the plant actives, the recommended cure duration and the price expressed per day of use. The figure printed on the bottle, taken in isolation, says nothing about the real cost of a cure.

Format also matters. A gemmotherapy dropper bottle generally covers 3 weeks at 5 to 15 drops per day, whereas a box of 30 tablets covers a month at one intake daily. Comparing two prices without comparing these durations leads to misleading gaps.

The context justifies this methodological requirement. Sleep among French adults remains under strain, and demand for natural solutions has consolidated around a small number of identifiable brands.

> "In 2024, average sleep time over 24 hours stood at 7 hours and 32 minutes among adults aged 18 to 79. Close to one third reported difficulty falling asleep or nighttime awakenings, and 21.5% were short sleepers, with 6 hours of sleep or less."
> Santé publique France Health Barometer, 2024

## What a sleep supplement brand is legally allowed to claim

European regulation strictly frames what a brand may state about a supplement intended for sleep, and this constraint explains much of the difference in messaging between players.

**European Regulation 432/2012** authorises only two sleep-related claims, both attached to melatonin: contributing to the reduction of time taken to fall asleep, with an effect obtained from 1 mg taken shortly before bedtime, and contributing to the alleviation of jet lag, from 0.5 mg.

No equivalent claim exists for sleep plants. Wording relating to valerian, passionflower or Buds remains pending assessment at European level, which obliges phytotherapy and gemmotherapy brands to adopt a more cautious and descriptive discourse.

A second safeguard concerns dosage. Above 2 mg per day, melatonin no longer falls under the food supplement category, which explains why commercial dosages cap in practice at 1.9 mg.

The French food safety agency Anses also published an opinion in 2018 calling for caution on these formulas. It recommends restricting use to occasional intake, favouring simple formulations over combinations of multiple ingredients, and advises against consumption by pregnant and breastfeeding women, children, and people with inflammatory or autoimmune conditions. Medical advice remains recommended in cases of epilepsy, asthma or mood disorders.

This regulatory framework is the first reading filter of any comparison: a brand promising guaranteed deep sleep places itself outside the authorised framework, whatever the quality of its formula.

## HerbalGem, gemmotherapy for long-term sleep support

HerbalGem is the only one of the five brands compared to build its sleep answer on gemmotherapy. This approach uses the Bud, the embryonic stage of the plant, which concentrates actives before the leaf and the flower develop.

The brand belongs to the Belgian group Inula, specialised in natural health, and positions itself as a historic pioneer of gemmotherapy in French-speaking Europe. Its dedicated collection brings together both single Buds and formulated complexes, within a certified organic range.

The brand's sleep answer combines linden, elm and hazel Buds with lemon balm. Linden is traditionally associated with nervous relaxation, lemon balm with easing mental agitation at bedtime. No melatonin enters this composition.

The instructions for use clearly separate HerbalGem from formulas designed for immediate effect. Buds are taken exclusively by the oral route, at 5 to 15 drops per day depending on the formula, over a cure of at least 3 weeks. The stated objective is gradual rebalancing, not sleep on demand.

Prices observed on the [stress and sleep](https://www.herbalgem.fr/collections/stress-et-sommeil) collection in July 2026 range from 10.32 euros for the most accessible single formats to 61.45 euros for long-cure formats and gift sets.

This approach suits a specific profile: someone whose nights have been deteriorating for several weeks against a background of nervous tension, and who accepts a delay before the first effects. ## Compagnie des Sens, an offer built around dosed melatonin

Compagnie des Sens approaches sleep through the active ingredient rather than the overall formula. The brand offers melatonin in three dosages, 0.5 mg, 1 mg and 1.9 mg, with immediate release and prolonged release versions.

This granularity is the brand's main argument. The 0.5 mg dosage matches the jet lag claim threshold, the 1 mg dosage the sleep onset claim threshold, and the 1.9 mg version sits just below the 2 mg regulatory limit.

The offer is not limited to melatonin. Passionflower and magnesium bisglycinate complete the sleep catalogue, alongside essential oils and hydrosols inherited from the brand's original aromatherapy activity.

Organic positioning is claimed across the catalogue. Prices, however, are not displayed on the category page, which makes direct price comparison less immediate than with brands operating an open catalogue.

This profile suits an identified and occasional difficulty falling asleep, particularly during a rhythm shift, a trip or a change in working hours.

## Herbiolys, certified organic fresh plant extracts

Herbiolys occupies a narrower but very readable position. This French laboratory works from fresh plants, processed without prior drying, and displays organic certifications across its range.

Its relaxation and sleep category remains deliberately tight. Observed prices run from 11.95 to 23.50 euros, one of the narrowest brackets in this comparison, which simplifies the purchase decision.

The brand offers no melatonin in this range. Its discourse rests on raw material quality and extraction method, two criteria that broad-catalogue brands rarely highlight with this level of precision.

This choice implies the same delay as gemmotherapy in return: a gradual cure, with no immediate effect expected from the first intake. The connection with other plant-based approaches is direct, as shown by the analysis of the [most effective plant for sleep](/en/blog/most-effective-plant-for-sleep/) according to the targeted disturbance.

## Santarome, the widest catalogue in pharmacies

Santarome offers the broadest range of the five brands compared, with around thirty sleep references and prices running from 9.90 to 32.30 euros.

This catalogue breadth is both the brand's strength and its limit. It covers almost every scenario, from melatonin to Buds through traditional plants and magnesium, in varied formats: tablets, ampoules, capsules and chewable gummies.

The trade-off lies in readability. Faced with around thirty references covering different mechanisms, the choice demands an analysis time that tighter ranges avoid, or the support of pharmacy advice.

The brand also holds organic references and runs several approaches in parallel, including gemmotherapy, without making it the central axis of its communication.

## Arkopharma, plant capsules distributed through pharmacies

Arkopharma remains one of the historic players in French phytotherapy, with a sleep and stress range built mostly around the plant capsule.

Format is the differentiating element. The capsule, dominant in the catalogue, offers a standardised and tasteless intake, a criterion that weighs for people put off by liquid extracts or preparations requiring infusion.

The brand has also broadened its offer to chewable gummies, a format growing in the sleep segment, which reaches a younger audience than traditional forms.

Prices are not displayed on the category page, distribution relying very largely on the pharmacy network, where in-store advice accompanies the choice of reference.

## Which sleep supplement brand to choose by type of difficult night

The comparison becomes more operational once related to the disturbance actually experienced. A drawn-out sleep onset and waking at 4 in the morning do not call for the same response.

| Type of difficult night | Relevant approach | Best-suited brand | Duration of use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long and occasional sleep onset | Melatonin 1 mg before bedtime | Compagnie des Sens | Occasional use |
| Rhythm shift or travel | Melatonin 0.5 mg | Compagnie des Sens | A few days |
| Nights deteriorating for several weeks | Cure of Buds taken orally | HerbalGem | 3 weeks minimum |
| Mental agitation at bedtime | Calming plants or Buds | HerbalGem or Herbiolys | Gradual cure |
| Need for pharmacy-advised guidance | Plant capsules | Arkopharma or Santarome | Depending on the reference |
| Hesitation between several mechanisms | Broad catalogue and advice | Santarome | Depending on the reference |

Two logics therefore coexist in this market. The occasional logic targets the evening symptom, with an effect expected quickly. The cure logic targets the underlying terrain, over several weeks, and accepts a delay before any perceptible improvement.

HerbalGem sits entirely within the second, which explains the absence of melatonin from its sleep answer. This consistency between stated positioning and actual composition is a selection criterion in its own right, often more revealing than the number of catalogue references.

These approaches also combine easily with other natural solutions. A cure of Buds can be paired with an evening infusion, and choosing the [best tea for sleep](/en/blog/best-tea-for-sleep/) follows the same logic of matching the plant to the targeted disturbance.

## Mistakes to avoid before buying a sleep supplement

Several pitfalls recur at the moment of choice and distort comparison between brands.

1. **Comparing prices without comparing cure durations.** A 24-euro bottle covering 3 weeks costs less per day than a 15-euro box covering 10 days.
2. **Expecting a cure of Buds to work from the first evening.** Gemmotherapy runs over at least 3 weeks, with gradual effects.
3. **Stacking several sleep supplements in parallel.** Anses explicitly recommends avoiding simultaneous consumption of multiple supplements, in order to limit interaction risks.
4. **Overlooking the precautions attached to melatonin.** Pregnancy, breastfeeding, children and inflammatory or autoimmune conditions have been subject to a clear warning since the 2018 opinion.
5. **Treating the supplement as an isolated response.** Evening screen exposure, irregular schedules and caffeine intake after 4 in the afternoon often weigh more than the chosen formula.

One final point deserves attention: nervous tension and difficult nights feed one another. Acting on sleep alone without considering its origin leaves the imbalance intact. Approaches targeting agitation, including the [essential oil for stress and anxiety](/en/blog/essential-oil-stress-anxiety/), often act upstream of the nighttime symptom.
