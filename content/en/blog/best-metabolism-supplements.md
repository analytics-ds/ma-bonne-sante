## How to compare metabolism supplements without getting it wrong

The metabolism category has organised itself around the chewable format and one headline active, apple cider vinegar. Behind a shared promise of balance and body shape, compositions diverge to the point that two seemingly similar products deliver neither the same quantities nor the same nutrients.

Four criteria allow an objective comparison of the **best metabolism supplements**: the real amount of active per daily serving, the presence of nutrients carrying an authorised claim, the sugar load across a long course, and the price expressed per day of use.

Daily serving is the parameter that distorts comparisons most. It runs from one gummy a day at Bears with Benefits to six at Goli, a one-to-six ratio that changes everything: the real duration of a container, the amount of active ingested and the sugar load.

Dosage transparency is the second filter. Of the four products compared, one publishes no quantified amount at all on its product page, which makes any comparison of composition impossible for the buyer.

### Why cost per day upends the ranking of prices

Once expressed per day of use, the order of listed prices reverses. The Goli jar, cheapest per unit, covers only 10 to 20 days given its three to six gummy serving, pushing the daily cost to between 0.80 and 1.60 dollar.

Bears with Benefits, with the highest jar price, falls to 0.50 euro per day on its 180-gummy format thanks to the single daily serving. Yves Rocher stays at 0.83 euro per day whatever the format, since the three-month course at 74.97 euros offers no discount over the one-month course at 24.99 euros.

## What a metabolism supplement is legally allowed to promise

European regulation strictly frames the effects a supplement may claim, and that grid clearly separates the arguments used in this category.

The annex to Regulation (EU) No 432/2012 reserves metabolism-related claims for specific vitamins and minerals. Chromium carries two, B-group vitamins cover energy-yielding metabolism and tiredness, while zinc and biotin cover macronutrient metabolism.

> "Chromium contributes to normal macronutrient metabolism"
> Regulation (EU) No 432/2012, annex of authorised health claims

On weight loss, the framing is tighter still. A single ingredient holds an authorised claim, glucomannan consumed as part of an energy-restricted diet, alongside meal replacements. None of the four products compared contains glucomannan.

This grid clarifies the compositions. Bears with Benefits rests its formula on vitamins B6 and B12, Yves Rocher on chromium and vitamin B6, EAFit on chromium dosed at 625 % of reference values, Goli on vitamin B12 and folic acid. These nutrients, and these alone, give each product a regulatory basis.

## Apple cider vinegar, what the regulatory framework actually says

Apple cider vinegar is the active around which this category was built. Its regulatory status deserves to be stated plainly, because it governs how the whole comparison should be read.

No health claim covering apple cider vinegar appears in the annex to Regulation (EU) No 432/2012, neither for weight loss, nor satiety, nor blood sugar, nor digestion. The ingredient may sit in a formula and appear on a label, but no effect can be legally attributed to it.

That absence has a direct consequence for reading dosages. Between the 250 mg a day at Bears with Benefits and the 3000 mg at Goli's high serving, the gap is one to twelve, yet it translates into no difference in regulatory status, since no effect is recognised at any dose.

Brand positions differ markedly here. Bears with Benefits places its product on digestion and balance, and lets vitamins B6 and B12 carry the claims. Yves Rocher, by contrast, credits apple cider vinegar with supporting body-shape goals and managing sugar cravings. EAFit frames its formula in an openly stated weight-loss logic.

One composition detail deserves attention on concentrated formats. Apple cider vinegar is acidic by nature, and prolonged consumption in concentrated form calls for the same caution as liquid vinegar regarding dental enamel. Chewable formats limit that contact, but the question remains open at high servings.

## Bears with Benefits, one gummy a day and published dosages

The Retrouvez l'équilibre product from the Munich-based brand, part of the Havea group, sells for 27.90 euros in 45, 90 and 180-gummy formats. The serving is one gummy per day, the only such case in this comparison.

The formula delivers 250 mg of powdered apple cider vinegar, 0.7 mg of vitamin B6 or 50 % of nutrient reference values, and 1.3 µg of vitamin B12 or 52 %. These two vitamins carry the claims for normal energy-yielding metabolism and the reduction of tiredness.

The apple cider vinegar content is the lowest of the four, and that point must be stated without hedging for anyone seeking the maximum dose. Set against the regulatory framework, the choice remains coherent: no dose of apple cider vinegar unlocks a claim, whereas vitamin dosage is precisely what governs the right to make one.

Two features stand out in practical terms. The single daily serving first, which simplifies adherence over a long course. The formats next, running up to 180 gummies, with a cost per day of 0.62 euro over 45 days, 0.56 over 90 days and 0.50 over 180 days, the lowest in the comparison.

The product carries no added sugar, is vegan, with a natural apple flavour and French formulation. The product page shows 4.95 out of 5 across 98 reviews, the largest review base of the four.

The brand also addresses metabolism with two separate products, which usefully completes the picture. Bye Bye Fringales, at 24.90 euros for two gummies a day, delivers 60 µg of chromium or 150 % of reference values, 83 mg of choline, 160 mg of carob and 80 mg of cinnamon. Matcha Métabolisme et Énergie, at 27.90 euros for one gummy a day, pairs 80 mg of matcha with vitamin B12 at 960 % of reference values, riboflavin, niacin and zinc.

## Goli, the highest apple cider vinegar content and 2 g of sugar per gummy

Goli has established itself as the global reference for apple cider vinegar gummies. Each gummy delivers 500 mg of apple cider vinegar, the highest per-unit content in the comparison.

The recommended serving is one to two gummies, three times a day, meaning three to six gummies daily. Apple cider vinegar intake therefore ranges from 1500 to 3000 mg per day, far above the other three products.

The formula is completed by 1.2 µg of vitamin B12, corresponding to 50 % of the US daily value, folic acid, plus 40 µg of beetroot and 40 µg of pomegranate, two amounts too small to weigh on the composition.

The sharpest contrast concerns sugar. Each gummy contains 2 g of cane sugar and 3.5 g of total carbohydrates, amounting to 6 to 12 g of sugar a day depending on the serving, in a product sold to support a body-shape goal. The other three are formulated without added sugar or with sweeteners.

Two contextual points matter for a French buyer. The brand's site displays prices in dollars, 15.98 dollars per jar against a 25.00 dollar list price, and announces free shipping for the United States only. In France the product therefore travels through marketplaces, with no official distribution.

The 60-gummy jar also covers 10 to 20 days depending on the serving, putting the daily cost between 0.80 and 1.60 dollar. The product is vegan, set with pectin, and gluten-free.

## Yves Rocher Laborantin, French retail without published dosages

The Laborantin range from Yves Rocher offers its Gummies Vinaigre De Cidre + at 24.99 euros for a one-month course, meaning 60 gummies at two per day, with two-month and three-month formats at 49.98 and 74.97 euros.

The formula combines apple cider vinegar, chromium, vitamin B6 and an orange fruit extract. The product page correctly credits chromium with maintaining normal blood glucose and macronutrient metabolism, and vitamin B6 with energy-yielding metabolism and the reduction of tiredness.

The limitation lies elsewhere, and it matters for a comparison of composition: **no quantified dosage is published** on the product page. Neither the amount of apple cider vinegar, nor of chromium, nor of vitamin B6 is stated, which prevents any quantitative comparison with the other brands.

The brand also credits apple cider vinegar with supporting body-shape goals and helping manage sugar cravings, two formulations that go beyond the register authorised for that ingredient.

The product is presented as free from added sugar, with a note that naturally occurring sugars remain, and the page warns that excessive consumption may have laxative effects. No customer review is published to date. The cost works out at 0.83 euro per day, identical across all three formats.

## EAFit Minceur 360, Morosil and chromium at 625 % NRV

EAFit, a French sports nutrition brand, offers its Minceur 360 Gummies at 24.90 euros, with a serving of one to four gummies a day, the full dose being four gummies.

Per four gummies, the formula delivers 400 mg of Morosil, a patented blood orange extract, 1000 mg of apple cider vinegar, 300 mg of fibre, 13 mg of cola nut extract including 5 mg of caffeine, and 250 µg of chromium or 625 % of nutrient reference values.

It is the most loaded formula in the comparison by number of actives, and the only one containing caffeine, a point worth knowing for a late-in-the-day serving. Chromium is dosed more than four times above the level Bears with Benefits uses in its Bye Bye Fringales product.

The commercial positioning is explicitly weight loss, presented as a slimming ally without frustration, combined with a balanced diet and regular physical activity. Of the three headline actives, only chromium holds authorised claims, Morosil and apple cider vinegar having none.

The page states neither the number of gummies per box, which makes cost per day incalculable, nor whether the product is vegan. The gummies are sweetened with maltitol syrup and sorbitol. A single customer review is published, at 4 out of 5.

## Which metabolism supplement to choose by profile

### For a long course with a simple routine

Bears with Benefits answers this profile most directly, with a single daily gummy, a sugar-free formula and a 180-day format at 0.50 euro per day. It suits anyone prioritising adherence and a legible composition over the raw quantity of active.

### For the highest apple cider vinegar dose

Goli remains the reference on that criterion, with 500 mg per gummy and up to 3000 mg a day. The counterpart comes in three parts: the sugar load, the number of daily servings and the absence of official distribution in France.

### For a conventional French retail purchase

Yves Rocher offers the simplest availability, in store and online, at 24.99 euros for a one-month course. The trade-off is the complete absence of published dosages, which makes it impossible to know what the gummy actually contains.

### For a multi-active slimming formula

EAFit offers the fullest composition, with Morosil, high-dose chromium, fibre and caffeine. That profile suits use framed by a diet and physical activity, and means accepting messaging that goes beyond the authorised claims framework.

## Mistakes to avoid before buying a metabolism supplement

1. **Confusing the presence of an ingredient with an authorised effect.** Apple cider vinegar and Morosil hold no authorised claim, whatever quantity appears on the label.
2. **Reading a jar price without checking the serving.** A 60-gummy jar covers 60 days at one gummy a day, but only 10 days at six.
3. **Overlooking sugars across a long course.** A base at 2 g of sugar per gummy represents 540 to 1080 g of sugar over three months at the recommended serving.
4. **Buying a formula whose dosages are not published.** Without quantified amounts, no comparison of composition is possible, and claim compliance cannot be verified.
5. **Ignoring caffeine in a slimming formula.** A cola nut extract delivers caffeine, and a late serving can weigh on falling asleep.
6. **Expecting a supplement to replace lifestyle.** Authorised claims cover contributions to normal metabolism, not a transformation of body shape, a framing close to the one set out in the guide on [how to cleanse your liver fast](/en/blog/cleanse-your-liver-fast/).
7. **Confusing digestive comfort with weight loss.** The two goals call for different formulas, as detailed in the article on [difficult digestion](/en/blog/relieve-difficult-digestion-naturally/).
8. **Forgetting the other levers in the category.** Water retention responds to different actives, detailed in the comparison of [natural water retention supplement brands](/en/blog/natural-water-retention-supplement-brands/), and the chewable format appears across other needs, as in the comparison of the [best immune gummies](/en/blog/best-immune-gummies-vitamin-c-zinc-d3/).
