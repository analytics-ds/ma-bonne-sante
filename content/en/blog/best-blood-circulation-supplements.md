### What actually separates two circulation ranges

The circulation aisle has one quirk: brands file products there that have nothing in common. A gel applied to the legs, an essential oil and an oral supplement all sit under the same heading, which inflates the catalogue on display. Fleurance Nature lists ten circulation products, including a cooling gel and two essential oils, which leaves seven actual supplements. Granions shows eight items, several of them three-month bundles of the same product.

Four criteria hold up in a comparison: the real depth of oral supplements, organic certification, the format on offer and the cost per course. The rest comes down to personal preference, mainly the choice between a dry capsule and a liquid ampoule.

## HerbalGem: gemmotherapy applied to circulation comfort

HerbalGem is a Belgian laboratory belonging to the Inula group, specialising in bud macerates since the 1980s. The brand does nothing but gemmotherapy, where the other four cover the whole of herbal medicine. That focus shows in the catalogue: [gemmotherapy for beginners](/en/blog/best-gemmotherapy-brands-beginners/) is treated there as a discipline in its own right, with single buds on one side and formulated complexes on the other.

The circulation range holds 21 references, the highest count of the five brands. Eleven single buds form its base: horse chestnut, hawthorn, hornbeam, dogwood, almond, alder, lemon, olive, rowan, sweet chestnut and ginkgo biloba. Alongside them, five complexes target specific needs, including VenaGem for the heavy legs sensation, Cordiagem on general circulation and GinkgoGem built around ginkgo. The [full circulation range is listed on the brand's site](https://www.herbalgem.fr/collections/circulation).

### What the range brings in practice

- **Full organic certification**: every circulation reference is certified, with no conventional product mixed into the aisle.
- **Oral use only**: bud macerates are taken as drops diluted in a glass of water, never on the skin. That rule applies to gemmotherapy as a whole and sets it apart from essential oils.
- **Flexible dosage**: 5 to 15 drops a day depending on the reference, which allows a course to be adjusted without switching products.
- **Consistent pricing**: from 12.11 euros for small formats to 23.50 euros for complexes, with no sharp jump within the range.

The weak spot lies elsewhere: gemmotherapy takes a little learning. Eleven different buds for a single need is a lot for a newcomer. Complexes exist precisely for that reason, and the [dangers and contraindications of gemmotherapy](/en/blog/gemmotherapy-dangers-and-contraindications/) are worth reading before any first course.

## What Arkopharma, Super Diet, Fleurance Nature and Granions put on the shelf

Arkopharma, a French laboratory based in Carros, works with whole plant powder in capsules. Its circulation range lines up 13 references, including organic Arkogélules of red vine leaf, horse chestnut, witch hazel, butcher's broom, olive and hawthorn, plus the Arkofluides liquid vials and the Veinoflux line. This is the market's entry point, with prices starting at 3.99 euros and topping out around 22.60 euros. The trade-off: organic certification covers only part of the catalogue, so each product sheet needs reading.

Super Diet, a French manufacturer with more than sixty years behind it, offers 12 circulation products with genuine format variety: ampoules, capsules, tablets and single doses. The organic Quatuor Circulation leads the range at 20.10 euros for 120 capsules or 17.30 euros for 20 ampoules. The brand covers ginkgo biloba, red vine leaf and horse chestnut, with prices dropping to 8.40 euros for ginkgo tablets.

Fleurance Nature, based in the Gers region, plays the fully organic card. Seven actual circulation supplements sit in the catalogue, including red vine leaf, witch hazel and cypress ampoules, red vine leaf with blackcurrant and grape marc capsules, horse chestnut and ginkgo biloba, between 11.50 and 25.30 euros. The catalogue is short, but coherent.

Granions approaches circulation through trace elements as much as through plants, with its Veinomix line. Only eight items, priced up to 33.90 euros for long-course formats. It is the most expensive brand of the panel on this segment.

Red vine leaf is the best documented plant of the group, and the only one shared by all five brands.

> "Traditional herbal medicinal product used for the relief of symptoms of discomfort and heaviness of legs related to minor venous circulatory disturbances."
> — European Medicines Agency, European Union herbal monograph on Vitis vinifera L., folium, adopted in 2010

Traditional use status means what it says: a long enough record of use, not a demonstration of efficacy through clinical trials. That distinction applies to every plant in the table above, whichever brand sells it.

## Which brand fits which situation

### For a first course, with no prior knowledge

A formulated complex removes the need to choose between eleven buds. VenaGem at HerbalGem or the organic Quatuor Circulation at Super Diet both fill that role, in a 17 to 20 euro bracket. Super Diet's ampoule format has one practical edge: the dose is ready, nothing to count.

### For the tightest budget

Arkopharma is unbeatable on entry price with its powdered plant capsules from 3.99 euros. Over a three-month course, the gap with a specialised organic range exceeds 30 euros. Worth checking product by product: the organic label is not systematic.

### For an organic requirement from end to end

HerbalGem and Fleurance Nature are the only two brands of the panel whose circulation aisle is certified in full. HerbalGem wins on range depth, Fleurance Nature on simplicity of choice.

### For a liquid rather than dry approach

Super Diet lines up ampoules, single doses and bottles, HerbalGem works with drops and sprays. Both approaches hold up equally well, the difference comes down to taste and habit.

## The mistakes that keep coming up

1. **Mixing up oral supplements and topical care.** A light legs gel and a bud macerate do not work in the same place. Buds are never applied to the skin.
2. **Stopping after five days.** A circulation comfort course runs for three weeks minimum, often three months for those sensitive to heat or to long hours standing.
3. **Stacking four products at once.** Red vine leaf in capsules, ginkgo in ampoules and a bud complex in parallel is the surest way to lose track of what is doing what, and to multiply precautions.
4. **Ignoring the warning sign.** A single leg that swells, feels hot or painful is not a supplement question. That calls for a doctor, without delay.
5. **Forgetting the rest.** Daily walking, leg position, heat exposure: a supplement supports habits, it does not replace them. People dealing with heavy legs often face [water retention](/en/blog/natural-water-retention-supplement-brands/) too, and both subjects are worth working on together.
