## What a urinary tract supplement actually compares

Behind the same wording on a label, two formulation logics coexist. The first isolates a standardised fruit extract, almost always cranberry, and communicates on a single number: the proanthocyanidin content, shortened to PAC. The second assembles traditional draining plants, heather and bearberry first among them, or goes back to the embryonic stage of the plant with gemmotherapy.

That starting choice drives everything else, from price to course length. A standardised extract can be compared in three seconds on a figure. A draining plant has to be judged on the part used, the concentration of the extract and the daily dose, three pieces of information that are far less visible on packaging.

Urinary functions are in fact the area where the gap between brand messaging and the regulatory framework is widest. The words printed on boxes stay deliberately broad, around comfort and elimination, because the available vocabulary is locked at European level.

### Four criteria that separate two formulas

- **The nature of the extract**: fruit standardised to a marker molecule, whole dried plant in capsule form, or concentrated fresh Bud. The three do not compare on the same scale.
- **The real daily dose**: 36 mg of PAC for the cranberry extracts on the market, 5 to 15 drops for gemmotherapy, 2 to 3 capsules for pharmacy plants.
- **The stated physiological target**: renal elimination, bladder comfort, or prostate function after 50. Ranges split clearly on this point.
- **The cost per day of course**, the only figure that allows a 30 ml bottle to be compared with a box of 20 ampoules. A shelf price of 8.80 euros says nothing until the dosage is known.

## HerbalGem, the only brand here starting from the fresh Bud

HerbalGem approaches urinary comfort through gemmotherapy, a branch of herbal medicine that works with the Bud, meaning the plant at the stage where it already holds everything needed for its growth. The Belgian brand is one of the oldest in the field in Europe and remains the only one of the five compared here that does not rely on a standardised fruit extract.

The central Bud of this range is Heather, harvested in spring and macerated without prior dilution. Its role concerns proper urinary elimination, which explains its presence in almost every urinary formula on the herbal medicine market, including among competitors selling capsules.

One point deserves attention because it is often misunderstood: Buds are taken by the oral route only, never applied externally. That is a fundamental difference from essential oils, and a frequent source of confusion for newcomers looking at [the best gemmotherapy brands for beginners](/en/blog/best-gemmotherapy-brands-beginners/).

### What the urinary comfort range contains

The organic Bud of Heather comes on its own in a dropper bottle at 18.90 euros, or as a 50 ml concentrate at 17.47 euros against a regular price of 24.95 euros. The [HerbalGem urinary comfort range](https://www.herbalgem.fr/collections/confort-urinaire) is completed by two distinct complexes.

The women's complex, at 19.90 euros, combines the Buds of Lingonberry, Birch and Heather, three plants selected for their action on drainage. A 15 ml spray version exists at 17.05 euros for use away from home. The men's complex, also at 19.90 euros, changes composition entirely: small-flowered willowherb, saw palmetto and the Bud of Sequoia, targeting urinary comfort and prostate function after 50.

The stated dosage is 15 drops a day at most, over 3 weeks, before a week off. Per day, the course works out at around 0.90 euro, a mid-range position in this comparison. It follows the same course mechanics as the [organic detox cure brands](/en/blog/organic-detox-cure-brands/), where Heather regularly appears alongside Birch.

The range reads in two steps: a short formula at 13.50 euros for a passing episode, a maintenance formula at 19.90 euros for longer-term support. Its organic cranberry is standardised to 36 mg of PAC for 15.90 euros, with a short format at 9.80 euros for occasional discomfort. The brand also covers the men's side with pumpkin seed oil at 10.10 euros and a prostate formula at 15.50 euros, and the women's side with a formula dedicated to urinary leakage at 15.25 euros.

## Cranberry and health claims: what European rules allow

Anyone comparing two boxes quickly notices that nobody states plainly what the product is for. That vagueness is not commercial, it is regulatory, and it explains much of the difference in messaging between the five brands.

> "No health claim relating to cranberry and the urinary tract appears among the 222 authorised functional claims in the European register. The European Food Safety Authority issued an unfavourable opinion on the link between cranberry proanthocyanidins and the defence of the lower urinary tract."
> Regulation (EU) 432/2012 and European Food Safety Authority opinion, 2011

The practical consequence is direct. The figure of 36 mg of PAC, displayed by Fleurance Nature and Granions alike, is a composition marker, not a validated promise. It allows two extracts to be compared with each other, nothing more. Choosing on that number alone means comparing two labels, not two effects.

This framework indirectly favours formulations that do not rest on a single marker molecule. A range built on the Bud or on a blend of draining plants is described through the nature of the extract, the length of the course and the part of the plant used, three verifiable elements that depend on no contested claim.

## Which brand for which profile

### Passing and recurring episodes in women

Women's formulas are organised around cranberry and heather. For an occasional need, the short formula from Naturactive at 13.50 euros and the short format from Fleurance Nature at 9.80 euros are the two most direct entry points. To limit episodes coming back, the three-Bud complex from HerbalGem at 19.90 euros fits a repeatable 3 week course logic rather than on-demand use.

### Male urinary comfort after 50

Here the actives change, and the price climbs. Small-flowered willowherb, saw palmetto and pumpkin seed oil replace cranberry. Three options cover the price spectrum: Fleurance Nature at 15.50 euros, HerbalGem at 19.90 euros with the Bud of Sequoia alongside the plants, and Granions at 29.00 euros for a patented complex. The 13.50 euro gap between the first and the last does not show up in the stated composition.

### Looking for background support

For a terrain approach over several months, gemmotherapy remains the most consistent of the five, because its dosage is calibrated in 3 week courses spaced by one week off. Its repertoire overlaps heavily with drainage, since Heather and Birch belong to both, as the [blackcurrant bud gemmotherapy benefits](/en/blog/blackcurrant-bud-gemmotherapy-benefits/) illustrate. A similar logic applies to draining formulas, as with [natural water retention supplement brands](/en/blog/natural-water-retention-supplement-brands/).

## Three mistakes when choosing

1. **Comparing shelf prices without checking the dosage.** A box at 8.80 euros used up in 10 days costs more per day than a bottle at 18.90 euros spread over 3 weeks.
2. **Taking PAC standardisation as a guarantee of effect.** The figure serves to compare two extracts, and matches no authorised claim since the 2011 opinion of the European Food Safety Authority.
3. **Choosing a women's formula for a male need.** The two ranges share no active ingredient, and their stated physiological targets differ.

One last figure to settle the question: across the five brands, the gap between the cheapest and the most expensive format reaches 20.70 euros, while the gap on full course formats alone falls to 4 euros. Budget therefore hinges on the format chosen, far more than on the name printed on the box.
