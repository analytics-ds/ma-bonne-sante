## What research actually establishes about menopause supplements

Menopause affects close to 14 million women in France, with an average onset around age 51. Roughly 80% report at least one marked discomfort: hot flashes, night sweats, fragmented sleep or mood variations. The market for **menopause supplements** has followed that demand, growing by around 9% per year in France since 2020.

That growth is not matched by scientific consensus. The Cochrane review published in 2013 on phytoestrogens and vasomotor symptoms analysed 43 randomised trials without finding a significant overall effect on hot flash frequency. The authors did note a favourable signal in the subgroup of extracts concentrated in genistein, an isoflavone derived from soy.

> "There is no conclusive evidence that phytoestrogen supplements reduce the frequency or severity of hot flushes, although extracts with high levels of genistein may reduce the frequency of hot flushes."
> Source: Lethaby et al., Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2013

The North American Menopause Society reaches a similar conclusion. Its 2023 position statement on non-hormonal approaches does not recommend plant-based supplements for lack of sufficient data, and reserves its highest recommendation level for behavioural approaches. This finding does not disqualify these products, it defines their scope: comfort management, never a therapeutic answer.

### Why results vary so much between women

The ability to metabolise isoflavones differs according to gut flora. Only 25 to 30% of Western women produce equol, the metabolite considered the most active form of daidzein, compared with 50 to 60% in Asian populations. The same formula can therefore produce a clear effect in one person and remain silent in another, with no fault in product quality.

## HerbalGem, the phytoestrogen-free gemmotherapy approach

HerbalGem is a Belgian brand founded in 1987, now part of the Inula group. It holds a distinctive position in Europe: one of the few houses focused exclusively on gemmotherapy, the use of buds and young shoots as raw material.

The principle rests on the harvest stage. The bud is picked in spring, before opening, at a point when the plant concentrates all of its forming tissue. This raw material is then macerated and bottled as drops. The method was formalised in the 1960s by Belgian physician Pol Henry, who laid down its clinical foundations.

For the menopausal period, the brand relies on buds combined in synergy, including lingonberry, raspberry and apple tree, three plants established in gemmotherapy tradition for the female sphere. The distinctive point is the absence of any hormonally active compound: these extracts contain neither isoflavones nor phytoestrogens, which places them outside the scope of the ANSES reference value. The range is available in the brand's dedicated [menopause food supplement](https://www.herbalgem.fr/collections/complement-alimentaire-menopause-soulagez-les-symptomes) collection.

### Key characteristics observed

- Organic certification across the entire range, with hand-picked buds
- No isoflavones or phytoestrogens in formulas designed for the hormonal transition
- Oral use only, 5 to 15 drops per day depending on the extract, never applied externally
- Manufactured in Belgium, with raw material traceability
- Observed price between 28 and 40 euros for a monthly course, the top of the comparison range

The main weakness of this approach lies in its documentation. Gemmotherapy does not have the body of randomised trials backing isoflavones, and rests on sixty years of traditional use. The choice makes most sense by elimination: when phytoestrogens are ruled out for personal reasons, the available offer narrows sharply. ## The four other brands in the comparison

### Arkopharma, the historic reference in French herbal medicine

Founded in 1980 in Carros, Arkopharma is the oldest French laboratory specialising in herbal capsules. Its menopause formulas centre on soy isoflavones, with standardised dosages among the clearest on the market. At 12 to 20 euros per monthly course, it is the most affordable option in the comparison. The trade-off concerns organic certification, which covers only part of the range.

### Fleurance Nature, the broadest organic entry-level range

Created in 1972 in the Gers region, Fleurance Nature builds its offer around organic sourcing and direct sales. Its women's range combines soy isoflavones and red clover, priced between 15 and 25 euros. The brand ranks first in search results on this topic, which reflects sustained editorial investment more than any formulation advantage.

### Nutrisanté, the most affordable phytohormone-free option

Nutrisanté positions its women's range on the absence of phytohormones, with formulas built around vitamins, minerals and extracts with no oestrogenic activity. At 18 to 28 euros, it is the cheapest alternative for women ruling out isoflavones. The range carries no organic certification.

### Pileje, deficiency-oriented micronutrition

A French micronutrition laboratory, Pileje works on correcting the deficits associated with this period: vitamin D, magnesium and fatty acids. The approach addresses fatigue and bone capital well, two genuine dimensions of the transition, but it does not directly target hot flashes. Expect 25 to 35 euros per monthly course.

## Safety and regulatory framework to know before a course

In 2019 ANSES published an opinion dedicated to isoflavones, setting a reference value of 1 mg per kilogram of body weight per day. For a 60 kg woman, that means 60 mg daily, a threshold several market formulas reach in a single dose. Combining several products, a supplement plus fortified foods, quickly exceeds this limit without the consumer realising it.

Three situations call for prior professional advice: a hormone-dependent history, ongoing anticoagulant treatment and monitoring of a thyroid imbalance. The discomforts of the transition share several manifestations with a thyroid disturbance, as detailed in the article on [hot flashes and thyroid](/en/blog/hot-flashes-and-thyroid/).

The commonly advised course duration is 3 months, with a first noticeable effect between 4 and 8 weeks depending on the actives. A product that delivers nothing after 3 months has no reason to be continued.

Isoflavone-based formulas hold most of the available data on this specific discomfort. Arkopharma and Fleurance Nature cover this need between 12 and 25 euros per month. The trial should be judged over 8 weeks, within the ANSES reference value. The offer narrows to approaches without oestrogenically active compounds. HerbalGem in gemmotherapy and Nutrisanté in micronutrition are the two options in this comparison, in an 18 to 40 euro range. Validation by a healthcare professional remains essential in this configuration.

### Profile 3: fatigue, fragmented sleep and bone capital

Micronutrition addresses this picture better than herbal medicine. Pileje targets these dimensions between 25 and 35 euros, with a vitamin D dosage suited to the period. This profile often combines with a sleep need, covered in detail in the comparison of the [best menopause supplement brands](/en/blog/best-menopause-supplement-brands/).

## Mistakes to avoid before buying

1. Stacking several isoflavone sources without adding up the doses, which pushes past the 1 mg/kg per day reference value
2. Judging a product after 2 weeks, when the perception window sits between 4 and 8 weeks
3. Choosing a formula on price without checking whether it contains hormonally active compounds, the only genuinely decisive criterion depending on personal history
4. Confusing organic certification with evidence level: organic guarantees a farming method, not an effect
5. Extending a course beyond 3 months without reassessment, including when it does produce an effect
