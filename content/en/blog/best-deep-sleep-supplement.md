## Deep sleep, what exactly is being discussed

Deep sleep, or slow-wave sleep, refers to a specific stage of the sleep cycle, the one in which brain activity slows the most and physical recovery is most marked. It is neither the total length of the night nor the ease of falling asleep.

That distinction is decisive when comparing supplements. A product that shortens sleep onset does not necessarily increase the share of deep sleep, and a product acting on the nervous system groundwork does not work through the same mechanism as a synchronisation hormone.

Four criteria allow an objective comparison of the **best deep sleep supplements**: the nature of the actives and their dosage, the existence of an authorised claim, the level of clinical evidence actually available, and the price expressed per day of use.

Format matters too. Immediate-release melatonin acts on entering the night, whereas an extended-release form targets sleep maintenance and mid-night awakenings, two distinct objectives that should not be conflated at the point of purchase.

## What a sleep supplement is legally allowed to promise

European regulation strictly frames the effects that may be claimed, and that framework explains most of the differences in messaging seen in this category.

The annex to Regulation (EU) No 432/2012 recognises only two sleep-related claims, both carried by melatonin.

> "Melatonin contributes to the reduction of time taken to fall asleep"
> Regulation (EU) No 432/2012, annex of authorised health claims, usable from 1 mg taken shortly before bedtime

The second claim covers the alleviation of jet lag, from 0.5 mg. **No authorised claim covers sleep depth**, slow-wave sleep, night-time awakenings or sleep quality in general.

Magnesium holds claims on the normal function of the nervous system and the reduction of tiredness, but none mentions sleep. The classic sleep plants, valerian, passionflower, lemon balm, hops and poppy, remain pending European assessment and hold no authorised claim.

A dose constraint applies in France: food supplements are capped below 2 mg of melatonin per daily dose, above which the product falls under medicinal regulation. The 1.9 mg used by Biocyte and Novoma is therefore the legal maximum. ANSES has also issued a vigilance opinion advising certain groups, including pregnant women and people on medication, to avoid these supplements.

## Melatonin, magnesium and glycine: what the clinical trials show

Beyond the regulatory framework, three actives concentrate the published data on sleep, with markedly uneven levels of evidence.

**Melatonin** is the best established on sleep onset, which is precisely what its authorised claim reflects. Its documented effect concerns sleep onset latency, not the proportion of deep sleep across the night.

**Magnesium** has been the subject of a reference meta-analysis. Mah and Pitre, in 2021 in BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies, pooled 3 randomised placebo-controlled trials totalling 151 older adults.

> "Sleep onset latency was 17.36 minutes shorter than placebo. The 16.06 minute gain in total sleep time did not reach statistical significance."
> Mah J, Pitre T, BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies, 2021

The authors add that the quality of the literature remains insufficient to ground well-informed recommendations. A more recent systematic review covering 12 randomised trials concludes on modest benefits for some subjective outcomes, with inconsistent results and low to very low certainty.

**Glycine** is the active with the most direct data on the deep stage. Yamadera and co-authors, in 2007 in Sleep and Biological Rhythms, showed by polysomnography that 3 g taken before bedtime shortened both sleep onset latency and the latency to slow-wave sleep, without altering overall sleep architecture.

This ranking of evidence does not match the ranking of claims: the active best documented on deep sleep holds no authorised claim, while the active that does hold one does not act on that stage.

## Biocyte, the only range covering sleep onset and the groundwork

The Nice-based brand, a nutricosmetics specialist, structures its sleep offering around the Noctrim range, complemented by a magnesium range acting on a different lever.

**Noctrim Sommeil**, at 16.00 euros for 30 capsules, a one-month course at 0.53 euro per day, combines 1.9 mg of melatonin, 56 mg of magnesium corresponding to 15 % of nutrient reference values, 0.59 mg of vitamin B6 or 42 %, 175 mg of poppy extract and 50 mg of inositol. The serving is one capsule, 30 minutes before bed.

**Noctrim LP**, at 19.90 euros, pushes the logic towards sleep maintenance. The formula keeps 1.9 mg of melatonin and 57 mg of magnesium, adds 120 mg of valerian, 112 mg of poppy and 1.4 mg of vitamin B6 at 100 % of reference values, all within an HPMC matrix releasing the actives **over 8 hours**. It is the only product in the comparison to state its release duration precisely.

**Magnesium Liposomal 300 mg**, at 26.00 euros for 60 capsules, provides 300 mg of magnesium, or 80 % of reference values, across three forms, oxide, carbonate and bisglycinate, combined with taurine and seven B-group vitamins. It is the only meaningful magnesium contribution in the whole comparison, since the other two brands offer none.

This double coverage is the range's central argument. Melatonin addresses the moment of falling asleep, magnesium addresses the nervous system groundwork over time, and only Biocyte offers both. The counterpart is clear: combining the two levers means two products, bringing the cost to around 1.40 euro per day.

A **gummy version** completes the range at 14.50 euros, with melatonin and vitamin B6, the lowest entry price in the comparison. Review volumes remain modest on the Noctrim products, 8 and 4 reviews, where Magnesium Liposomal shows 67 for a rating of 4.64 out of 5.

## Novoma, extended-release melatonin on the longest course

Novoma's Formule Sommeil sells for 23.90 euros for a 45-day course, or 0.53 euro per day, with a serving of two capsules an hour before bed and French manufacturing.

The formula combines 1.9 mg of melatonin in the Melotime extended-release form, the same regulatory ceiling as Biocyte, with a complex of five plants including lemon balm, passionflower, valerian and hops, dosed from 100 to 200 mg.

The brand explicitly claims a sleep maintenance objective, on the grounds that immediate-release melatonin helps falling asleep without acting on mid-night awakenings. The argument is sound in substance, but the release duration is not quantified on the product page, where Biocyte states 8 hours.

The absence of magnesium is the main limitation with respect to deep sleep, since that is precisely the active covered by the 2021 meta-analysis. The brand does sell magnesium bisglycinate elsewhere in its catalogue, but not within its sleep formula.

The 45-day format is the longest in the comparison and is a practical advantage on a maintenance course, at a daily cost identical to Biocyte's Noctrim Sommeil. The brand communicates on a consumer test run with 45 people, which is a satisfaction test rather than a controlled clinical trial.

## Nutri&Co, betting on a melatonin-free formula

Nutri&Co offers its Sommeil formula at 19.90 euros, with a serving of two capsules an hour before bed. The stance is the opposite of the other two brands: **no melatonin at all**.

The formula rests on 485 mg of DailyZz, a complex combining 388 mg of spearmint extract and 97 mg of green tea extract, on 30 mg of Safr'Inside saffron standardised to 0.6 mg of safranal and 0.9 mg of crocins, and on 75 mg of GABA. The product is vegan, gluten-free and lactose-free.

This positioning answers a real demand, that of people wishing to avoid the hormone, whether for a contraindication, medical advice or personal preference. The approach echoes the one detailed in the comparison of [natural sleep supplement brands](/en/blog/natural-sleep-supplement-brands/).

The counterpart is twofold. By forgoing melatonin, the formula forgoes the only active holding an authorised sleep claim. And with neither magnesium nor glycine, it mobilises none of the two actives documented by the clinical trials cited above.

The exact container format is not published on the page consulted, which prevents calculating a cost per day and comparing the real course duration with the other two brands.

## The missing active: glycine

The observation deserves to be stated plainly, because it is counter-intuitive. **None of the three brands compared offers glycine**, although it is the active whose polysomnographic data bear most directly on slow-wave sleep.

The 2007 Yamadera study remains the reference on the subject, with a 3 g serving before bedtime and objective measurement by polysomnography. Other Japanese work, notably Inagawa in 2006 and Bannai and Kawai in 2012, reports improvements in perceived sleep quality and morning fatigue.

Three limits should be flagged. Sample sizes in these trials are small, around twenty participants. The effective 3 g dose is far above what a standard capsule can hold, which requires a powder format. And glycine holds no authorised claim, just like the sleep plants.

For a buyer whose goal is specifically sleep depth, this is worth knowing: it means turning to an amino acid sold on its own, outside the sleep formulas on the market.

## Which supplement to choose by type of difficult night

### For a long time falling asleep

Melatonin is the only active holding an authorised claim on this precise point. Biocyte's Noctrim Sommeil and Novoma's Formule Sommeil are both dosed at 1.9 mg, the legal maximum, for an identical daily cost of 0.53 euro.

### For mid-night awakenings

Extended release targets this use. Biocyte's Noctrim LP states a release duration of 8 hours via an HPMC matrix, where Novoma claims an extended form without specifying its duration.

### For a fragile nervous system over time

Magnesium belongs to a groundwork logic, and Biocyte is the only one of the three brands to offer it, at 300 mg or 80 % of reference values. The 2021 meta-analysis data bear precisely on that active.

### For avoiding melatonin

Nutri&Co is the only option built without the hormone, around saffron, GABA and spearmint. That choice suits anyone with a contraindication or a firm preference, accepting the loss of the only active covered by a claim.

## Mistakes to avoid before buying a sleep supplement

1. **Confusing sleep onset with sleep depth.** Melatonin acts on how long it takes to fall asleep, and no authorised claim covers slow-wave sleep.
2. **Believing a higher melatonin dose will work better.** The legal ceiling for a supplement is below 2 mg per day in France, and the claim applies from 1 mg.
3. **Taking plants for validated actives.** Valerian, passionflower, lemon balm, hops and poppy hold no authorised claim, their applications remaining pending European assessment.
4. **Overstating the evidence for magnesium.** The reference meta-analysis covers 3 trials and 151 subjects, with a significant effect on sleep onset latency alone and a level of evidence its own authors judge low.
5. **Ignoring the ANSES vigilance opinion.** Certain groups, including pregnant and breastfeeding women and people on medication, should avoid melatonin supplements.
6. **Expecting a supplement to replace sleep hygiene.** Regular hours, light and bedroom temperature remain the primary determinants, as detailed in the guide on [how to treat insomnia naturally](/en/blog/how-to-treat-insomnia-naturally/).
7. **Forgetting non-pharmacological approaches.** Evening infusions are a complementary ritual, detailed in the comparison of the [best tea for sleep](/en/blog/best-tea-for-sleep/), and the plants involved are reviewed in the article on the [most effective plant for sleep](/en/blog/most-effective-plant-for-sleep/).
