## How to compare immune gummies on an objective basis

The immune aisle has thickened in chewable format, and formulas sold under the same natural-defences promise rest on widely different compositions. All three products compared pair micronutrients with a plant active, but in different proportions and with different logics.

Four criteria allow an objective comparison of the **best immune gummies with vitamin C, zinc and D3** without being led by the packaging: the nature of the nutrients and their dose relative to reference values, the number of actives genuinely carrying an authorised claim, the daily serving required, and the price expressed per day of use.

Serving size remains the most decisive parameter. Two products out of three require two gummies per day, which halves the apparent duration of the container. A 60-gummy jar then covers one month, and a 42-gummy jar three weeks.

The announced trio also deserves a line-by-line check. Of the three products, only Fleurance Nature genuinely combines vitamin C, zinc and vitamin D3 in a single gummy. Mium Lab contains no vitamin D. The Totem d'immunité from Bears with Benefits contains no zinc, the brand offering it as a dedicated product.

## What an immune gummy is legally allowed to promise

European regulation draws a precise line around the effects a supplement may claim, and that constraint clearly separates the arguments used in this category.

The annex to Regulation (EU) No 432/2012 lists eleven authorised claims relating to the immune system. They cover ten nutrients: copper, iron, folate, selenium, vitamin A, vitamin B6, vitamin B12, vitamin C, vitamin D and zinc.

> "Vitamin D contributes to the normal function of the immune system"
> Regulation (EU) No 432/2012, annex of authorised health claims

The most useful observation concerns plants. No authorised claim covers elderberry, echinacea or propolis, whose applications remain pending European assessment. These ingredients may appear in a formula and on a label, but no immune effect can be legally attributed to them.

This grid clarifies the compositions. All three products carry a plant active of that kind, in widely different amounts: 80 mg of echinacea at Mium Lab, 30 mg of elderberry at Fleurance Nature, 10 mg of propolis at Bears with Benefits. The latter therefore rests its formula on micronutrients rather than on the plant share, with four nutrients carrying an authorised claim against three for the other two.

A further condition governs the use of these claims: the product must be at least a source of the nutrient concerned, meaning 15 % of the nutrient reference values for the daily serving. All three products meet that threshold for several nutrients.

## The trap of 100 % reference values for vitamin D

A figure of 100 % of nutrient reference values suggests full coverage of the requirement. The reality is more nuanced, and this point concerns all three products in the comparison.

The reference value used for nutrition labelling is set at 5 µg of vitamin D per day. The dietary reference retained by the French agency ANSES for adults stands at 15 µg per day, a value defined by considering dietary intake alone, without counting the contribution of sun exposure.

A product showing 100 % of the labelling value therefore provides roughly one third of that reference. Bears with Benefits provides 5 µg, Fleurance Nature 2.5 µg, Mium Lab none.

> "More than 70 % of French adults show vitamin D insufficiency, with outright deficiency in 6.5 % of cases."
> ANSES, reference data on vitamin D

This reading does not invalidate the value of the gummies, but it places their role: a contribution among other intakes, not a sufficient answer where insufficiency is established. A blood test remains the only way to establish an individual status.

## Mistakes to avoid before buying immune gummies

1. **Confusing the presence of an ingredient with an authorised effect.** Elderberry, echinacea and propolis hold no authorised immune claim, whatever quantity appears on the label.
2. **Reading a jar price without checking the daily serving.** Two products out of three require two gummies per day, which halves the duration of the container.
3. **Taking 100 % of the vitamin D reference value for full coverage.** The labelling reference is 5 µg where the ANSES reference for adults is 15 µg per day.
4. **Checking the announced trio line by line.** Only one of the three products combines vitamin C, zinc and vitamin D3 in a single gummy.
5. **Confusing sugar-free with no added sugars.** The first excludes sugars, the second only signals that none were added, naturally occurring sugars remaining in the product.
6. **Stacking several supplements containing zinc.** The tolerable upper intake level is set at 25 mg per day for adults, including pregnant and breastfeeding women. The immune gummies compared stay between 2.5 and 5 mg, but stacking can approach that limit.
7. **Expecting a supplement to replace lifestyle.** Sleep, diet and physical activity remain the primary determinants, as detailed in the guide on how to [strengthen the immune system naturally](/en/blog/strengthen-immune-system-naturally/).
8. **Ignoring complementary approaches.** Other families of natural solutions lend themselves to seasonal use, such as the [blackcurrant bud in gemmotherapy](/en/blog/blackcurrant-bud-gemmotherapy-benefits/).
